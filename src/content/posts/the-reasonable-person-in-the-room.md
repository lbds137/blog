I am so fucking tired. On Wednesday, my French teacher from high school posted an essay on Facebook about a boy who plays on a girls' field hockey team in Massachusetts. He finds this absurd, and he uses it to get to his real subject, which is whether transgender women should be allowed to compete in women's sports. It is at least his sixth essay on that subject since January 2022. I used to look up to him as a reasonable, intellectual guy, but I just can't deal with this. He does know about me (he was my teacher before I transitioned), and he knows we're friends on Facebook, so this especially pisses me off.

I'm not going to name him or link to his essay, because this post ultimately shouldn't be directly about him. It's about how liberals repeatedly fail trans people over and over again while patting themselves on the back for being so thoughtful. He gets to be the case study because he won't shut up about the topic. The timing helps, too. Today is Friday the 13th, Transgender Awareness Week starts today, and yesterday marked eight months since my surgery.

## He already won

I should be fair to him first. In 2020 he called the Trump administration's rollback of health care protections for trans people "heartless bigotry" (my comment under that one was "It's depressingly exhausting existing as a trans person in the current political climate.", and it still is). He calls me Lena, and he writes "she" about the trans women in his essays. His argument is that the physical advantages of male puberty can persist after hormone treatment, that fairness to cis women is a legitimate interest, and that in our position he would step aside. As he put it on Wednesday, "If I were a transgender woman, would I insist on competing against cisgender women knowing that I retained a significant competitive advantage? I'd like to think not."

I'll concede the science as far as it goes, because I can't honestly claim there is no advantage. A [2021 review of the research](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/33648944), led by Joanna Harper (who is a trans woman herself), found that hormone therapy brings strength and muscle mass down, but that they "remain above those of cisgender women, even after 36 months". The studies are small and mostly aren't of athletes, but that's where the evidence stands. The public is with him as well. In a [Pew survey](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/02/26/americans-have-grown-more-supportive-of-restrictions-for-trans-people-in-recent-years/) from February 2025, 66% of U.S. adults favored requiring trans athletes to compete on teams that match their sex assigned at birth.

Here is what he doesn't seem to have noticed, though. He won. The NCAA [limited women's sports](https://www.ncaa.org/news/2025/2/6/media-center-ncaa-announces-transgender-student-athlete-participation-policy-change.aspx) to athletes assigned female at birth in February 2025, the day after Trump's executive order on the subject. This March the International Olympic Committee [announced](https://time.com/article/2026/03/26/olympics-ban-transgender-athletes-womens-events-policy/) a one-time genetic test for the female category, starting with the 2028 Games. In June the Supreme Court [upheld](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/609us2r68_hgci.pdf) state laws that keep trans girls and women off girls' and women's teams. According to the [Movement Advancement Project](https://mapresearch.org/equality-map/bans-on-transgender-youth-participation-in-sports/), 29 states now have laws that do that, and two more do it by regulation. So when he signs Wednesday's essay "One man's opinion, expressed once again", it is one man's opinion plus the Supreme Court, the Olympics, the NCAA, and 31 states. (Claude, the AI I write these posts with, did the digging on where the rules stand, and this point came out of that.)

He is living in a pre-Trump era when it comes to sports. He keeps arguing about something that has already been settled in a way that hurts trans people, and he keeps trying to act like he's the reasonable person in the room. Bro, no the fuck you're not. In December 2024, the president of the NCAA [told a Senate committee](https://www.wate.com/news/nexstar-media-wire/ncaa-president-says-there-are-less-than-10-transgender-athletes-in-college-sports/amp/) that out of about 510,000 college athletes, he was aware of fewer than 10 who were transgender.

## The foot in the door

Honestly, I have purposely avoided thinking too hard about the sports thing, because I have never given a shit about sports. Well, okay, that's not actually true. I got into running and I got obsessed with it (I ran 3,237 miles in 2014, and some of the [oldest](/blog/posts/my-one-year-runniversary/) [posts](/blog/posts/running-mile-in-my-shoes/) on this blog are about it), but I didn't get into running to try to win anything. I wanted to beat what I could do as a person. My running went to complete shit during my transition.

Trans exclusion is happening in more than just physical sports anyway. In August 2023 the international chess federation, FIDE, [ruled](https://handbook.fide.com/chapter/TransgenderRegulations) that a player who changes gender from male to female "has no right to participate in official FIDE events for women" until its council decides otherwise. Wow, are we just so much more big-brained than cis women that we can't play chess? The same rule also takes women's titles away from trans men.

I already know what my French teacher thinks about this one, because he wrote it down. His June 2022 essay says that women's sports "exist solely because of anatomical, physiological, and endocrinological differences between the genders". Then he asks, "Why do women compete separately in chess?" His answer is "The assumed inferiority of women in chess has baffled me and seems to echo the eugenic arguments used to justify racism." A [2009 study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2679077) of German players found that 96 percent of the gap between the top 100 men and the top 100 women is what you would expect from how many more men play. So chess has no anatomy to argue about, and a little over a year after he wrote that, trans women were shut out of women's chess anyway. As far as I can tell, FIDE never gave a fairness reason, and he never wrote an essay about it. (Claude found the chess paragraph when I asked it to look.)

A big part of sports isn't even the competition. It's how a lot of people socialize and build community, and given how obsessed society is with sports, playing is a crucial part of social participation in general. In Kentucky, a trans girl [helped build](https://spectrumnews1.com/ky/louisville/news/2022/06/13/what-s-next-for-a-kentucky-student-who-testified-against-trans-sports-bill) her middle school's girls' field hockey team from the ground up (field hockey, same as Wednesday's essay), and then the state's 2022 ban kept her off the team she helped start. It's too easy to just say, well, they should just not play sports. The right wing wants us sidelined from public existence, and that's what the bathroom bills are really about, because if you can't even go take a piss anywhere that's not your home, you can't be outside as much. They do not want us to be visible. They want to indoctrinate their kids into a very cis, heteronormative, patriarchal view of things, and trans people complicate that. Kids are smart, so the parents have to go extra hard. This might be slightly conspiracy theory thinking on my part, but I really don't think it's a stretch.

Sports is also how the right wing gets its foot in the door and tries to sound reasonable to the public. Bathrooms actually came first. North Carolina passed its bathroom bill in 2016, and the bathroom provisions were repealed a year later. According to a [2023 New York Times report](https://www.nytimes.com/2023/04/16/us/politics/transgender-conservative-campaign.html), conservative groups went looking for a new approach after that. One of them polled voters in 2019 and found that a ban on trans girls in school sports went over better than a bathroom ban, and in the Times's words, conservatives "decided to focus on two main fronts", which were sports and medical care for minors.

So sports was the foot in the door, and the rest has followed. [26 states](https://mapresearch.org/equality-map/bans-on-best-practice-medical-care-for-transgender-youth/) now ban transition care for minors, the Supreme Court upheld Tennessee's ban last year, and a federal rule that took effect in October bars Medicaid money from paying for that care for anyone under 18. The right wing already moved past the sports issue for the most part, and they're working on taking away our health care. Thankfully, they have overplayed their hand, and that is probably going to eventually lead to more trans acceptance, because the right wing is being so absolutely shitty to us that even "reasonable" people who are paying any degree of attention are hopefully going to have their hearts softened. That will come at the cost of trans lives and trans suffering, though, so that's just fucking great.

## The baker

Wednesday's essay also stops at a bakery. If he were gay, he writes, he'd like to think he would take his business away from a homophobic baker (the Supreme Court [sided with one](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/16-111) in 2018), because "There are other bakers." Then he asks, "Would we insist that a Jewish baker decorate a cake with swastikas? Of course not."

The comparison is pretty ridiculous, and I would imagine it's intentionally so. But right-wing people are actually flashing swastikas around. Elon Musk gave a straight-arm salute on stage, twice, at Trump's inauguration rally, and [people are still arguing](https://www.yahoo.com/news/musk-says-gestures-trump-inauguration-012702042.html) about what he meant by it. It's not an absurd example, actually. It comes off as tone deaf and incredibly privileged, and my French teacher knows about privilege. In 2020 he wrote that he had been "given an EZ-Pass at birth", and that "As an old white guy, it would be presumptuous of me to claim that I get it. But I do get that I can't get it."

Gay couples are about love, about trying to build a life together. You're putting that next to swastikas, which are literally about fascism and ethnic purity, about not wanting white people to get replaced by Jewish people, people of color, queer people, or disabled people. In Charlottesville, they [chanted](https://www.splcenter.org/resources/hatewatch/when-white-nationalists-chant-their-weird-slogans-what-do-they-mean/) "Jews will not replace us." The comparison says a lot more about him as a person than he would like it to. He thinks he's just doing it for the sake of argument. No, dude, the fact that you're even bringing this up is a black mark next to your character.

And why is the baker the one who has to be protected? He acts like he's not picking a side, but if you read between the lines, he very clearly is, and it's the side of the privileged person who probably isn't being discriminated against in lots of other parts of his life. If you're a minority, the government is going out of its way to write laws to make your life shittier, and on top of that, you have people refusing you service. These are not isolated incidents. And what if you live in a small town and there's only one baker?

When it's a job instead of a cake, especially with the job market right now, discrimination could be the difference between life and death. A [2013 review of the research](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0051333) found that the risk of suicide was about two and a half times higher in the first five years after someone became unemployed, and a [2015 study](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2215-0366(14)00118-7) of 63 countries linked over 40,000 suicides a year to unemployment. I have had suicidal thoughts as a direct result of being fired or laid off, that little voice in the back of my mind saying, well, I guess you're not worth anything. At least to my knowledge, none of my job losses were discriminatory, and each one was still a potentially life-ruining event. And the Trump administration [ended](https://www.npr.org/2025/07/16/nx-s1-5467516/trump-administration-ends-988-lifelines-press-3-option-for-lgbtq-teens) the option on the 988 suicide lifeline for LGBTQ+ young people in July 2025, saying it had "run out of congressionally directed funding".

Bigots love to wave around the [41 percent](https://newsroom.ucla.edu/releases/study-suggests-link-between-experiences-249952) figure, the share of trans people in a 2014 study who said they had attempted suicide. That study linked those attempts to "rejection by family and friends, discrimination, victimization, or violence". In 2021, a troll in a Discord server I owned posted a meme about a "transgender battalion" taking "40% casualties", then wrote, "40% is rooky [sic] numbers let's pump those numbers up" and "I don't see an issue with mocking suicide".

## I told him this already

None of the sports stuff should be news to him, because I said some of it in the comments under that June 2022 essay. I was still trying to write formally back then, so bear with me. I told him that "Due to... shall we say, a general lack of patience to attempt 'scientific' discussion (read: scrutinizing the minutiae of our... nonstandard bodily configuration), I'm not really inclined to weigh in on this topic beyond pointing out the unfortunate fact that transgender men are so often left out of the conversation, most likely due to society's obsession with transgender women as some sort of spectacle to gawk at and engage in 'fun' thought experiments about". I also told him that "the voices of cisgender women who are more than okay with competing alongside transgender women are often not given sufficient airtime either". He replied that he didn't see how trans men "would be relevant in this context". So I left him four links (from the ACLU, NBC Sports, ABC News, and NPR), and he answered, "I haven't read EVERYTHING you sent".

In November 2024, he wrote another essay on the subject, asked his readers to "please point me to evidence to the contrary", and ended it with "Change my mind." In the comments on that one, trans men had become his "yardstick". I figured he was probably just drawing agreement from people, which spreads this crap further. Claude went back through the comments, and that is mostly what it found. "At long last an honest man", one of them says. As far as I can tell, I'm the only one who ever pushed back on the whole exercise.

## Liberal, not woke

He is firmly a liberal rather than a progressive, from what I can tell, and I think he'd agree. In 2024 he wrote that a newspaper column had made him "certain I am NOT 'woke'". He has written that being told "they are" can be singular "feels like gaslighting to me", and he ended that one with "When the right denounces the wokeishness of the left, this is what they're talking about.. And you know, I hear them."

When I think about liberal versus progressive, I think of the chasm between the Hakeem Jeffries / Chuck Schumer / Nancy Pelosi side of the Democratic Party and the Bernie Sanders / AOC / Abdul El-Sayed / Ilhan Omar wing. Claude checked, and that chasm doesn't show up in the votes. Nobody on either list voted for the federal sports ban. It shows up in how they talk about us. In January 2025, AOC [said on the House floor](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2025-01-14/html/CREC-2025-01-14-pt1-PgH126.htm), "Trans girls are girls". Two months later, Jeffries answered a question about trans athletes with "What Democrats opposed was unleashing sexual predators on girls throughout the United States of America." They were against the same bill, but only one of those sentences has a trans person in it. For what it's worth, the Democrats my French teacher has written essays in support of are Seth Moulton and Gavin Newsom.

## Newsom

Newsom is the one that disturbs me. In March 2025 he opened his new podcast by [sitting down with Charlie Kirk](https://www.nbcnews.com/nbc-out/out-politics-and-policy/california-gov-gavin-newsom-breaks-democrats-trans-sports-participatio-rcna195165), a man who opposed same-sex marriage and talked about an "LGBTQ agenda". Kirk asked him, "Would you say no to men in female sports?" Newsom answered, "I think it's an issue of fairness, I completely agree with you on that," and then, "It is an issue of fairness — it's deeply unfair." Later he said, "I am not wrestling with the fairness issue. I totally agree with you." Newsom hasn't moved since. This September he [told the AP](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/some-democrats-recalibrating-approach-to-transgender-issues), "I haven't been able to figure out how to make it fair from my perspective."

My French teacher wrote his Newsom essay the day the episode came out. He called Newsom "the very incarnation of woke" and said the fairness position "suffers the uncomfortable inconvenience being true, at least for now". He called trans women in women's sports "a marginal social issue", and said Democrats "could shed a few of their aspirational ideals in favor of uncomfortable truths". The governor of the biggest blue state went on his own podcast and agreed with Charlie Kirk about us, and the reasonable liberal in my Facebook feed agreed with the governor.

My French teacher is also a fan of the New York Times, the same publication that has repeatedly thrown trans people under the bus. Three days after the Newsom essay, he wrote that the Times has been around long enough that "any deviations from perceived fairness have been ironed out by its rich and enduring history." In 2023, over a thousand of its own contributors [signed a letter](https://nytletter.com/) saying it had run over 15,000 words of front-page coverage debating medical care for trans kids in eight months, and that Arkansas's attorney general had cited three Times articles in a brief defending Alabama's ban on that care. (Yes, I cited the Times earlier. A paper with a bad pattern can still get a story right.) I don't think that's a coincidence.

The pat on the back is the part that gets me the most. In June 2024, a trans woman won the Miss Maryland USA pageant, and he wrote an essay about it. He opens by admitting that when he tries to accept trans women in women's sports, "I find myself rationalizing rather than justifying". He asks himself whether he is "a latent sexist whose bigotry, when push comes to shove, emerges", cheers her on with "You go girl!", and then says what pleases him most. "I am most pleased that I feel a bit more confident of the integrity of my argument against the participation of trans-women in women's sports." (Note the "trans-women", and elsewhere "transgendered" and "transculture". Cis and trans should be adjectives describing the flavor of womanhood, not a "normal" woman and a "transwoman".) A trans woman won a pageant, and what he took from it was that his own position checks out.

## Five messages

Before he wrote essays about trans women in general, he wrote to me. He asked me in 2018 to keep our messages between us, and I did that for eight years. I think it's reasonable to ask for protection for things that are genuinely private. But if you lean on privacy as a way to have shitty views in private without people knowing in public that you're kind of a bigot, I don't think that's a valid use of privacy. Ultimately this is about accountability, especially when people think that they're morally correct and go on being windbags, writing all these "oh, look at me, I'm so intellectual" posts, and then there's just zero fucking empathy whatsoever.

In April 2018, about a year and a half into hormones, he offered me "a word of advice". He was "intimidated both by the content and by the tone" of my Facebook posts, and he wrote, "Accepting you now as a woman isn't easy, but it's doable. Accepting your rage and very public and profane display of it is more difficult." A few days later it was "I knew you as a brilliant young man. I would hope your transition would yield a brilliant young woman. Instead, I find a rebel lashing out". That December my posts were "angry and entirely confined to gender issues", and he worried I was "blocking yourself off completely from the cis world". In January 2019 he worried about my "isolating yourself in transculture" and hoped I'd find my way back to "something approximating the mainstream". In February 2020 he was "disappointed that ALL of your posts deal with gender politics", and he told me that "constant raging against social injustice will convert no one and alienate potential allies."

He wasn't wrong that I was angry, or that I was profane about it. In the month before that last message, I posted "Rot in jail you fucking bigot." about a man convicted of a hate crime for beating a trans woman who used the women's bathroom, and "Fucking Nazi-tier tactics." about the South Dakota House passing a bill to restrict medical treatment for trans kids. It was never all of my posts (I told him it was probably 40 percent gender stuff at most, and when Claude went back and counted that month, it came to about 30 percent). A month after he warned me about alienating potential allies, Idaho passed the first sports ban. Less than two years after that, he posted his first essay about trans women in sports. The man who was disappointed that all of my posts dealt with gender politics has now written at least six of them about us. (Claude pointed that one out.)

I also feel like this connects to feminism. Men are allowed to get angry, and women are told that we're just shrill bitches. There is a double standard here, and it pisses me off. There's still this kind of centering of cis men being so rational. History is full of examples of cis men acting heavily out of anger or indignation, and they get to get away with that, but when women are emotional, we're just hysterical. Like, fuck off. That's bullshit, dude.

I'm sure he has been pissed off about stuff, too, and maybe he had the restraint to not post about it publicly. But he's trying to whitewash himself as purely an intellectual with these posts, and I think that's disingenuous. Everybody gets angry at some point or another, and it's really more about what you do with that anger. Is it channeled productively? Or, like the anger of Trump voters, is it channeled toward hating on a specific minority just because we're an easy target, because we're not palatable enough to be worth defending to most people? I think my anger is justified. In fact, I think my anger level right now is actually less than it should be.

## He mattered to me

I also have to admit how I responded at the time, which was to try to keep him. I asked him, "what can I do to alleviate the discomfort my public social media activities have caused you?" I told him I would feel "a significant sense of loss" if we stopped interacting. The one place I held my ground was when I told him, "I don't think that being "a brilliant young woman" and a "rebel" are mutually exclusive. I have a backlog of emotion to process that had been buried for a long while." To be fair to him again, the same thread has years of lunches and French in it. He checked in on me ("Thinking about you. Are you OK?"), and he called me "one of the most intelligent people ever to grace my classroom".

The reason I tried that hard is that he mattered to me. Given the absence of my father through a good chunk of my life, this teacher served a father figure role in absentia. Even my mom counted him, in 2017, among the "adults who care about you, who saw you growing, who want you well". My parents took my transition badly (I mentioned their "concerns and pleas" when I [came out](/blog/posts/the-2017-reboot/) on this blog). I went no contact with my dad for two years before he kind of started to come around, but then he died, and I don't feel like I got all that much closure, other than watching Hazbin Hotel and losing my shit over the Lucifer episode. And that's what fucking broke me. Lucifer's daughter, Charlie, got to have catharsis and reconciliation with her father, and those are not options for me. My dad's actually dead, so that's a thing, and any pseudo-father figure has gone to shit. That is a good part of what I feel like I've lost in all of this.

My mom is very much about her public image, and she got very pissy when I posted screenshots of her bullshit on Facebook. What put my teacher off the most in 2018 was, in his words, "the public shaming of your parents". I told him at the time that she had expressed very similar views, and some of his comments still remind me of her, especially the self isolation thing. Six months before his "brilliant young man" message, she wrote that I had a "double personality: Vlad the loving and respectful son, and Lena, a crazy bitch". His rhetoric is so fucking similar.

Actually, there's another teacher of mine, a Catholic, who unfriended me over Satanism. He mentored me on college stuff. In October 2018, he asked me about my "Satan related content", which was a Discord server I'd set up. I told him it was a discussion forum, not a cult. He wrote, "sorry, I'm not cool with that", and then, "Ok, but bye for now". That evening I commented, "I think that's the first Facebook friend I am aware of having lost directly as a result of my beliefs. Oh well, I am who I am."

My French teacher called my religion "a disturbing choice" in 2018 and told me, "Cards on table: I suspect Lilith is a phase." So I made not one but two adult men uncomfortable with my spiritual path. I'm not anywhere near as upset with the other teacher, because, you know what, he's a Catholic, and that's fine. I'm willing to be reasonable. I just think that past a certain point, being "reasonable" is just self-erasure, and not being allowed to have a reasonable reaction to a fucked-up situation.

Lilith isn't a phase, by the way. She has a very well-documented mythology of saying no to men trying to make her less than in some way, and honestly, this post and the anger I have about all of this is an excellent devotional exercise to her. I guess I found my rage this morning. My French teacher picked a great day to fucking piss me off.

## Shallow acceptance

This February, Facebook surfaced one of his posts on my news feed, so I said hello. The hello went unanswered. I don't think there's much value to trying to keep him.

For Transgender Awareness Week, someone on r/asktransgender asked what we would most like cis people to be aware of. One of the answers, from u/Dr-Satan, was a single line. "'I support trans people' is the whole sentence." My French teacher has been adding to that sentence since 2018.

At this point, I don't think there's anything he can really do to fix this, considering he keeps posting about trans people, and again, we're his thought piece. Whatever history I had with him has been rendered irrelevant by me transitioning. To me, that's yet another one of those things trans people give up when we choose to be our true selves. We give up this shallow acceptance and find ourselves isolated. He called that isolating myself. And it's not just because we're angry. I mean, that's part of it, but people never stop to think about why we're angry. They don't give a shit, and that's the problem. If people actually cared, we wouldn't have to get this fucking angry.
